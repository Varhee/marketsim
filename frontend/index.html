    01 What actually is an order book?

    It's two queues. People who want to buy stack up on one side, people who want to sell stack up on the other, sorted best price first. Nothing matches until someone's price crosses the other side's price. Try it: click a resting order below to see who it'd trade against.

    02 Why market makers matter more than they look like they should

    A market maker just quotes a price a few pence either side of "fair" and profits from the gap. That sounds passive, but they're the reason a big sell order can actually execute at all — without someone resting a bid, there's nothing for a seller to sell into. That also means they're the reason a shock can move price so fast: they're absorbing the trade, and their own risk limits are what eventually stop them.

    03 Why backtesting is easy to get wrong

    The gap between "my strategy returned 400%" and "my strategy returned 8%" is usually not the strategy — it's a mistake in how it was tested. A few of the most common ones:

    • Look-ahead bias — using information that wouldn't have existed yet at that point in history.
    • No out-of-sample data — tuning parameters on the same period you're testing them on.
    • Ignoring transaction costs — spread and slippage eat into returns that look great on paper.
    • Overfitting — a strategy with enough tunable parameters can fit noise, not signal.
